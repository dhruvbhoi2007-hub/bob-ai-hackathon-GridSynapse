# Problem Statement

## The Problem

Power transformer and substation failures cause blackouts costing utilities over $1 million per hour and disrupting service for millions of people. Despite this, most utilities still rely on **fixed, calendar-based maintenance schedules** — inspecting equipment on a set timetable regardless of its actual condition.

This is a mismatch with reality. Utilities already collect rich sensor data — temperature, vibration, partial discharge, and oil quality — that shows failure signatures **weeks in advance**. But this data is rarely combined with weather forecasts, even though extreme heat, high winds, and storms are known to accelerate equipment failure and compound existing weaknesses. As a result, utilities are often reacting to outages after they happen, instead of preventing them.

## Who Is Affected

- **Utility operations and maintenance teams**, who must decide — often with incomplete information — which of hundreds of assets to prioritize for inspection with limited crew time and budget.
- **Millions of residential and commercial customers**, who experience unplanned outages that a predictive approach could have caught in advance.
- **Utility companies financially**, who bear the direct cost of emergency repairs, regulatory penalties, and reputational damage from large-scale outages.

## Why Existing Approaches Fall Short

- **Calendar-based maintenance** treats all assets of the same type identically, regardless of their actual health — a 5-year-old transformer in perfect condition gets the same attention as a 20-year-old one showing early warning signs.
- **Sensor monitoring systems**, where they exist, typically report raw readings without contextualizing them against weather risk or translating them into a clear, prioritized action plan a crew can actually use.
- **Weather and grid-health data are managed by separate teams and separate systems**, so the compounding risk of "already-degraded equipment + incoming storm" is rarely caught in time to act.

## Why This Problem Matters Now

Grid infrastructure is aging in most regions at the same time that extreme weather events are becoming more frequent and severe. The combination of aging assets and intensifying weather risk means the cost of staying reactive — rather than predictive — is only going to grow. A system that can synthesize sensor health, weather forecasts, and asset criticality into a single, explainable, prioritized action plan directly addresses this widening gap.

## What OutageIQ Solves

OutageIQ combines asset sensor data with live weather forecasts to identify which grid assets are most likely to fail, explain **why** in plain language, and generate a prioritized maintenance and crew deployment plan — turning reactive maintenance into proactive risk management.
